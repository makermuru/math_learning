750 metres is 750 out of 1,000 metres. That is 0.75 of a kilometre.

Build the idea, one step at a time

If 1,000 metres makes one whole kilometre, then 750 metres is most of that kilometre, but not all of it.

Decimals show parts of a whole

Think of 1 whole kilometre as a strip split into 100 equal parts.

Look at the hundred-square

Each tiny square is 0.01. 75 shaded squares make 0.75. The same amount is 750 metres out of 1,000 metres.

Grade 3 Measurement, step by step

Read one idea, then practise it in the ⭐ Practice tab.

📏 Length: centimetres and metres

1 m100 cm
50 cm½ m
2.5 m250 cm

Line the zero of the ruler up with the start of the object. Estimate first, then measure. A metre stick is a good benchmark: a door is about 2 m tall.

➕ Perimeter: walk around the shape

Perimeter = add the lengths of all the sides.
A rectangle 4 cm by 3 cm: 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14 cm.

Check that you counted every side once. Use the same unit for every side.

⚖️ Mass: grams and kilograms

1 kg1,000 g
500 g½ kg
2 kg 250 g2,250 g

Grams are for small things (a pencil). Kilograms are for heavy things (a bag of rice).

🥛 Capacity: millilitres and litres

1 L1,000 mL
250 mL¼ L
1.5 L1,500 mL

Millilitres measure small amounts (a spoon of juice). Litres measure big amounts (a milk jug).

🕒 Time: clocks and elapsed time

1 hour = 60 minutes. Count the minutes from the start time to the end time.
From 2:40 to 3:15 is 35 minutes.

Count in jumps: 2:40 → 3:00 (20 min) → 3:15 (15 min) = 35 min.

🔢 Decimals in measurement

0.1 m = 10 cm (one tenth of a metre). 0.01 m = 1 cm (one hundredth).
So 1.36 m = 1 m 36 cm.
